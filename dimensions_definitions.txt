# ===== HUMANITARIAN EVALUATION DIMENSIONS (EXAMPLES) =====
Sector|||Sector: the specific humanitarian sector or domain that the research addresses, such as health, nutrition, shelter, education, protection, water and sanitation, or food security. Papers are classified based on the primary sector they contribute to or evaluate.
Modality|||Modality: the type or form of humanitarian intervention being evaluated, such as cash transfers, in-kind assistance, capacity building, service delivery, advocacy, or protection services. This dimension captures how aid is delivered and what mechanisms are used.
OutcomeDomain|||OutcomeDomain: the specific area of impact or change being measured in humanitarian evaluation, such as livelihoods, resilience, well-being, program effectiveness, behavioral change, or system strengthening. This focuses on what outcomes are being assessed.
EvaluationType|||EvaluationType: the methodological approach used for evaluation, such as impact evaluation, process evaluation, formative evaluation, summative evaluation, real-time evaluation, cost-effectiveness analysis, or mixed-methods evaluation. This dimension categorizes papers by their evaluation methodology.
CrossCuttingThemes|||CrossCuttingThemes: overarching themes that cut across multiple sectors, such as gender equality, disability inclusion, climate adaptation, conflict sensitivity, localization, accountability to affected populations, or environmental sustainability. These are horizontal concerns that apply across different interventions.
GeographicContext|||GeographicContext: the geographic setting or context of the evaluation, such as urban vs. rural, fragile states, protracted crises, natural disasters, conflict zones, or specific regional contexts (e.g., Sub-Saharan Africa, Middle East, Asia-Pacific). This captures where evaluations take place.
ActorType|||ActorType: the type of organization or actor conducting or being evaluated, such as UN agencies, international NGOs, local NGOs, government agencies, private sector actors, community-based organizations, or consortia. This identifies who is involved in the humanitarian response.
ResourceInputs|||ResourceInputs: the types of resources being provided or evaluated, such as financial resources, human resources, material goods, technical assistance, information systems, or infrastructure. This dimension focuses on what inputs are being delivered.
EvaluationCriteria|||EvaluationCriteria: the evaluation criteria being applied, based on frameworks like OECD-DAC criteria (relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, impact, sustainability, coherence) or humanitarian-specific criteria (coverage, appropriateness, protection, participation). This captures the lens through which evaluations are conducted.
CoordinationArchitecture|||CoordinationArchitecture: the coordination mechanisms and structures in place, such as cluster system, inter-agency coordination, cash working groups, local coordination forums, or government-led coordination. This examines how different actors work together.
EvidenceType|||EvidenceType: the type of evidence being generated or used in the evaluation, such as quantitative data, qualitative insights, participatory data, administrative data, satellite imagery, social media data, or indigenous knowledge. This categorizes papers by their evidence base.
RecommendationType|||RecommendationType: the nature of recommendations or findings produced, such as policy recommendations, programmatic adjustments, methodological innovations, scaling strategies, or advocacy priorities. This captures the actionable outputs of evaluations.
